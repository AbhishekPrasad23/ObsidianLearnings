

The term **P99 metrics** refers to a performance measurement used in systems and software engineering, particularly in the context of latency and response times. Based on the surrounding LinkedIn post, it was mentioned during a backend interview at a FAANG company, highlighting its importance in high-scale, high-performance environments.

### 🧠 What Are P99 Metrics?

- **P99** stands for the **99th percentile** of a dataset.
- In latency terms, it means that **99% of requests complete faster than this value**, and only 1% are slower.
- It’s a way to measure **tail latency**, focusing on the worst-performing requests rather than the average.

### 📌 Context from the Post

The candidate was asked: _“How would you improve the P99 metrics?”_

- His initial response was to improve the **SLA (Service Level Agreement)** of microservices.
- When challenged further, he proposed a clever solution: **send two requests with a slight delay and cancel the slower one**.
    - This technique is known as **hedging requests**, used by companies like Google to reduce tail latency.

### 🚀 Why P99 Matters

- Average latency can hide performance issues.
- P99 highlights **outliers** that affect user experience, especially in real-time systems.
- Improving P99 is crucial for systems that require **consistency and speed**, like financial platforms, search engines, or ride-sharing apps.
